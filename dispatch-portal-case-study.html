One live board for every load.

How I replaced spreadsheets and group texts with a dispatch portal that a small freight team uses every day.

The problem

Loads were tracked in spreadsheets, phone calls and texts. To answer “where is this load?” a dispatcher had to find the right text thread or call the driver. Paperwork came back late, and nobody had one clear picture of the day.

My role

I designed, built and launched it myself, from the first prototype to the live system, and I keep it running. I also handled the dispatchers’ feedback after launch, including status names, time formats and a fuel surcharge field.

How a load moves through it

  1. Load createdA dispatcher enters it once on the shared board.
  2. Driver assignedA text alert tells the driver the details.
  3. Status updatesThe driver shares status and location from the phone.
  4. PaperworkDelivery documents are uploaded from the cab.
  5. Everyone sees itDispatchers work at once and see the same live board.

Simplified workflow for illustration. No customer data is shown on this page.

What it does

For dispatchers

  • One shared board for loads, drivers and status
  • Text alerts to drivers
  • Several dispatchers working at the same time
  • Simple color-coded statuses

For drivers

  • A mobile app for iPhone and Android
  • Check in and share location
  • Send delivery paperwork from the cab

How I built it

It started as a single-file prototype and grew into a production system once the team began relying on it. Along the way I fixed problems that only real use reveals, such as two dispatchers editing the same load, and I reviewed the database access rules for security.

Results

It has been in daily use since May 2026 and the team now works from one board instead of texts. I have not measured time saved yet, so I am not publishing any numbers. When I can verify them, they will go here.

Could this fit your business?

The same approach works for any team that tracks jobs in spreadsheets and texts: delivery, field service, stores or a small warehouse. I would start by mapping how work moves through your day, then build the smallest version that removes the most manual steps.
